# PHASE 3 — WORLD BIBLE: THRENYX

> Status: **LOCKED** (gate at bottom).
> Season 1: **FIRSTFALL**.
> Sections marked **◼ SEALED** are the studio's authored truth. They are never published directly — they leak into the game through card flavor, Legacy titles, event text and season reveals. Everything outside SEALED sections is what the people *of* Threnyx believe, and they don't agree.

---

## 0. The pitch in five lines

Threnyx is a world that is a song — specifically, a **funeral song**, still being sung.
Twenty-one years ago a weapon **unsang** a city: erased it from the song, from maps, and from every mind.
The backlash burned an empire to ash. Terrified, the survivors outlawed war and swore to settle everything — crowns, debts, murders, borders — by **Clash**: duels between squads of eight Champions called **Octaves**.
Every night the world falls silent for a while. That silence — **the Hush** — is getting longer.
And last night, for the first time in recorded history, **a star fell**.

That is **Firstfall**.

---

## 1. The core idea: a world made of sound

The single organizing idea behind names, magic, factions, UI sound and card design:

**Everything in Threnyx is a note in the Threnody.** People, cities, rivers, oaths. Stars are sustained notes held in the sky (the **Choir**). To be forgotten is to be *unsung*. To die is to *fall silent*. Powerful people are *Attuned*: they can hear their own note and push it.

This gives the product a coherent language:
- **Octave** — a Clash squad of eight (in-world term; UI says "Octave" with "8 Champions" underneath until learned).
- **The Hush** — nightly silence. Also the **audio design rule**: the moment before a Mythic reveal, the game *goes silent*. The Hush is diegetic.
- **Orders as music.** STRIKE is *forte* — overwhelming force. GUARD is *sustain* — holding your note against theirs. SCHEME is *counterpoint* — playing the note that makes theirs sound wrong.
- **READ** — Threnyx calls it *hearing the next note*. Duelists say "I heard you." The game says **READ ✓**.
- **Writs.** A Champion fights for whoever holds their **Writ** — a legal contract under the Accord. Writs are bought, sold, inherited and stolen. *A card is a Writ.* Trading cards is diegetic commerce; Legacy is a Writ's chain of custody.

## 2. Cosmology — four accounts, no canon answer

Nobody in Threnyx knows who is singing. The four living accounts:

**The Vigil's account — *The Lament.*** Before the world, something beloved died. A Voice began to mourn it, and the mourning *is* the world. As long as the Voice sings, we exist. The Hush is the Voice drawing breath. It is lengthening because the Voice is tiring. When the breath becomes longer than the song, the world ends. *Duty: keep singing so the Voice does not sing alone.*

**The Hourwrights' account — *The Mechanism.*** There is no Voice. The Threnody is resonance — a measurable vibration in all matter, as lawful as a bell. The Hush is a standing wave, an interference pattern, and interference can be engineered. *Anything that can be measured can be owned.*

**The Margin's account — *The Rehearsal.*** The song isn't a lament for something that died. It is a lament for *us*, sung in advance. The world is mourning itself before its death. Which means the ending is already written, and the only free people are the ones who have been erased from the score.

**The Quiet's account.** The Quiet do not preach. When asked, an Arbiter will ring a wax-stopped bell once and say nothing. Their founding charter contains one cosmological sentence: *"There is a rest written in the score. We are its keepers."*

**◼ SEALED — what is true.** All four are partly right. There is a Voice, it is mechanical *and* grieving, and the Hush is a written rest — but it is lengthening because the Unsinging tore the score. The world is trying to heal the missing bar. Firstfall is the first stitch.

## 3. The map

Threnyx's known continent is **the Meridian**, a long crescent around an inland sea called **the Stillwater** (it has no waves; sound does not carry across it).

| Region | What it is | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **Cinderhold** | Mountain fortress where the Ashen Crown rules in exile. Built from the black glass of Solenne's burning. | Mourning cathedral, obsidian, ember-light |
| **Solenne (the Ashfields)** | The burned imperial capital. Ash still falls here, twenty-one years later. No one knows why. | Grey snow that isn't snow |
| **Coda** | Neutral city on the Stillwater where the Accord was signed. Seat of the Quiet. Clashes of record are fought here in the **Stillhouse**. | Black stone, silver, total silence in the streets |
| **Carillon** | Canal city of the Hourwrights. Ten thousand bells, each tuned to a sold hour of someone's life. | Verdigris, brass, amber gaslight, rain |
| **Lanternfast** | Monastery-city of the Vigil, built as a lighthouse spiral. Never dark, never quiet. | Indigo night, candle-gold, choirs |
| **The Blank** | Where the Unsung City was. On every map it is white space. Walk in and you begin forgetting things — first names, then faces, then why you came. | Paper-white fog, ink-drip ruins |
| **The Margin** | The ragged settlements along the Blank's edge: **Scrawl**, **Rulings**, **Last Legible**. Home to people who were forgotten, or who want to be. | Patchwork, cartographic tattoos, violet lamplight |

The Firstfall star came down **inside the Blank**. Nobody who holds a map can find it. The Margin can.

## 4. History (Accord Years; "BA" = Before Accord)

| When | Event |
|---|---|
| Myth | **The Overture.** The song begins. Every faction dates it differently. |
| ~1200 BA | **The Twelve Choirs.** City-states grow around *tuning stones* — rocks that hum. Each city's identity is literally its pitch. |
| ~300 BA | **The Choir-Throne.** The Vantaine dynasty unites eleven Choirs from Solenne. The twelfth refuses. It will one day be unsung. |
| ~120 BA | **The First Hush.** Scholars record one second of silence at midnight. **The Vigil** is founded to sing through it. |
| ~60 BA | **Tolling discovered.** Carillon's clockmakers learn to draw hours of life out of a person's note and store them in bells. The **Hourwrights** become the empire's bankers. The poor begin selling years. |
| 12 BA | **The Unended Night.** The Vigil prophesied the world would end when the Hush reached thirty minutes. It reached thirty minutes. The world didn't end. The Vigil split; the survivors of the schism became harder, not softer. |
| 6–2 BA | **The War of the Last Verse.** The Choir-Throne marches on the twelfth city. The Hourwrights sell to both sides. |
| 2 BA | **The Unsinging.** The Hourwright weapon called the **Stilling Fork** is struck at the twelfth city. The city, its people and its name are erased from the Threnody — and from every memory. The **Recoil** rolls back along the Fork's note and burns Solenne to ash in one night. **Empress Maelis Vantaine vanishes.** |
| 2–0 BA | **The Grey Years.** Famine, warlords, no empire. People trade lives in hours. |
| 0 AY | **The Accord of the Octave.** Signed at Coda. No armies, no sieges, no wars. Every dispute — succession, territory, debt, crime — is settled by Clash between Octaves of eight Champions, fought until one side's Voice falls silent. **The Quiet** are founded to judge Clashes and to hold what remains of the Stilling Fork. |
| 0–19 AY | **The Accord Years.** A generation grows up where politics *is* dueling. Champions become celebrities. Writs become the most valuable objects in the world. |
| **19 AY** | **FIRSTFALL.** A star of the Choir — a sustained note that has hung in the sky since the Overture — goes out and falls into the Blank. It is the first star to fall in recorded history. Something walked out of it. |

## 5. The central mystery: **What happened at the Unsinging?**

This is the franchise's "who shot first" argument. Every faction has an account, each is supported by at least one piece of in-game evidence, and each is wrong about something.

| Account | Claims | Evidence players will find |
|---|---|---|
| **Crown (official)** | The twelfth city was building its own Fork to unsing Solenne. The Crown struck first. The Recoil was Vigil sabotage. | Aurelle's card flavor; Crown propaganda banners in the Vault's Crown page |
| **Hourwright ledger** (leaked page) | Empress Maelis signed the firing order. | The signature is dated **one day after** she vanished. |
| **Vigil sermon** | The twelfth city sang a false note. The Threnody corrected itself. The Fork was only the instrument of judgment. | High Cantor Absolom's card; the Vigil's missing schism records |
| **Margin testimony** | The city *chose* to be unsung, to escape the empire forever. Its king stayed behind to remember it. | The Hollow King's card text; Vega's maps show streets *still being built* in the Blank |

**Three hard questions fans should argue about:**
1. Who struck the Fork?
2. Why did the Recoil burn Solenne, when the Fork was designed not to recoil?
3. The Accord has **eight seals** but lists **seven signatories**. Who signed the eighth — and why can no one remember?

**◼ SEALED — what actually happened.**
- The twelfth city had **no weapon**. The Crown's intelligence was forged by Guildmaster **Ottavio Brask**, who wanted the Fork used once, so its terror would make Hourwright neutrality priceless forever.
- Empress Maelis intended to fire the Fork herself and take the guilt. The night before, the Fork's designer, **Ilsabet Brask** — Ottavio's wife — was found dead in a locked bell tower (unsolved; see §8).
- **Aurelle Vantaine, seventeen, commanding at the front, struck the Fork** — before her mother could arrive — because a forged dispatch said the city would fire at dawn. She has hidden this for twenty-one years and built her entire regency on the official account.
- The Recoil happened because the Fork had **three tines** and one was missing. Ottavio had removed the third tine to sell it. Without it, the note came back.
- The eighth seal was signed by the Hollow King. Every signatory saw him sign. All of them forgot.
- Maelis survived, burned, took a vow of silence, and founded the Quiet. She is **the First Silence**. She now judges her own children's Clashes.

## 6. Magic and technology

**Attunement.** Some people can hear their own note. With training, they can push it: to strike harder than a body should (forte), hold ground impossibly (sustain), or bend another's intention (counterpoint). Champions are, almost by definition, the Attuned.

**Rules of Attunement** (to keep power stories grounded):
- You can only push your *own* note. Nobody controls another person directly.
- Pushing costs something: breath, sleep, memory, years. Characters who use too much become strange.
- The Hush suppresses everything. No one is Attuned in the Hush. That's why Clashes are forbidden during it.

**Tolling.** Hourwright surgeons draw hours of life from a person's note and seal them in bells. A tolled hour can power a machine, heal a wound, or be sold. The rich live long. The poor sell decades. Automata run on tolled time — which means every machine in Carillon is, in a sense, alive with somebody else's years.

**The Stilling Fork.** A tuning fork the height of a cathedral, with three tines, designed to cancel one note absolutely. The Quiet hold two tines in the Stillhouse vaults. **The third tine is missing.**

**Resonance weapons.** Swords are tuned. A duelist's blade is forged to their pitch and hums in their hand. When two tuned blades cross, they create a beat frequency — spectators say they can *hear* who's winning.

## 7. Factions

Five factions for launch. Each has a philosophy that is *right about something*, a silhouette readable at thumbnail size, a colour language, heroes and villains, and a crack running through the middle of it.

### 7.1 THE ASHEN CROWN
**"Duty outlives the realm."**
- **Who:** the Vantaine court-in-exile at Cinderhold. Nobility without land, knights without a war, a dynasty without an empire, all bound by ritual mourning that has never ended.
- **Colour:** bone-white, ash-grey, ember-red. **Symbol:** a crown drawn as a ring of flame with one tine missing.
- **Silhouette:** tall. Burnt-iron halo crowns, long mourning veils, ash smeared on the face in the shape of a hand (the Mourner's Hand). Cloaks that trail like smoke.
- **Architecture:** black volcanic glass fused from Solenne's burning; cathedrals, reliquaries, hearths.
- **Combat culture:** formal, ceremonial, disciplined. Losses are honoured. Fallen Champions' names are read aloud every night.
- **Mechanical identity:** *legacy & revenge*. Crown Champions draw strength from the Spent — the fallen empowering the living — and hit back harder after losses.
- **The crack:** **Restorationists** (take the throne back — by Clash if possible, by the Fork if not) versus **Mourners** (the empire is dead; honour it and let it rest).
- **Heroes:** Aurelle Vantaine, Sir Hadric Moor, Tamsin Ashby. **Villains:** Corvin Vantaine, the Cinder Widow.

### 7.2 THE QUIET
**"Judgment is silent."**
- **Who:** the order that judges every Clash and enforces the Accord. They take vows of silence and wear bells with the clappers removed. Their Champions — **Silencers** — fight Clashes of punishment against Accord-breakers.
- **Colour:** black, cold silver, white. **Symbol:** a bell without a clapper.
- **Silhouette:** narrow and vertical. Blindfolds or eye-veils, high collars, ledgers chained to the waist, silent bells.
- **Architecture:** the Stillhouse at Coda — enormous, geometric, acoustically dead. You cannot hear your own footsteps.
- **Combat culture:** patience, observation, punishment of predictability. The Quiet watch every Clash in the world. They know everyone's tells.
- **Mechanical identity:** *information & prediction*. Quiet Champions learn the enemy hand, punish READs, and control Initiative.
- **The crack:** the Quiet are supposed to be neutral. They are not. Their leader is a Vantaine (SEALED), and their vaults hold two-thirds of a doomsday weapon.
- **Heroes:** Arbiter Senna Loe, Wren. **Villains:** Ossian Drae, the Tongueless. **Unknowable:** the First Silence.

### 7.3 THE HOURWRIGHTS
**"Everything has a price. Especially time."**
- **Who:** the clockmaker-merchant guild of Carillon. They built the Stilling Fork. They bank the Accord. They buy years of people's lives.
- **Colour:** verdigris green, brass, amber. **Symbol:** a bell whose tongue is a clock hand.
- **Silhouette:** mechanical asymmetry. Lensed goggles, brass prosthetics, greatcoats with ticking pockets, one sleeve always rolled for the tolling needle.
- **Architecture:** canals, bell towers, glass arcades, factories of ticking bells.
- **Combat culture:** contracts, leverage, efficiency. They fight for pay and want receipts.
- **Mechanical identity:** *Focus economy*. Hourwrights buy, bank, steal and multiply Focus.
- **The crack:** **Guildmasters** (time is property) versus **the Unwound** (a movement of freed hour-debtors who smash tolling bells).
- **Heroes:** Imre Vask, Tock. **Villains:** Ottavio Brask, Hesper Quill.

### 7.4 THE MARGIN
**"What is forgotten is free."**
- **Who:** the people living along the Blank. Survivors whose families were half-erased, runaways, heretics, criminals, and people who walked into the Blank on purpose. Some of them are *partly unsung*: you forget their faces within minutes.
- **Colour:** paper-white, ink-black, one saturated violet. **Symbol:** an ink blot shaped like a door.
- **Silhouette:** patchwork, asymmetry, cartographic tattoos, faces with missing features drawn in as ink lines.
- **Architecture:** scaffolding built into fog; towns made from scavenged fragments of places nobody remembers.
- **Combat culture:** improvisation, misdirection, unfairness. They don't care how the Accord says to fight.
- **Mechanical identity:** *bluff & unpredictability*. Margin Champions reward weak-Order plays, are hard to READ, and bend Clash rules.
- **The crack:** do they want to *remember* the lost city — or stay free of it?
- **Heroes:** Vega Sorn, Dask Holloway. **Villains:** Mother Ink. **Both:** the Hollow King, Nyx.

### 7.5 THE VIGIL
**"Sing, so the Voice is not alone."**
- **Who:** a militant faith that believes the Threnody must never stop, and so never stops singing. Their knights carry lanterns and stay awake through the Hush for years at a time. Since the Unended Night, they are fewer, harder, and angrier.
- **Colour:** midnight indigo, candle-gold. **Symbol:** a lantern containing an open eye.
- **Silhouette:** broad and luminous. Lantern-halberds, eyes painted open on the eyelids, choir-robes worn over plate.
- **Architecture:** Lanternfast, a lighthouse spiral city that has not been dark for 120 years.
- **Combat culture:** faith, endurance, momentum. They sing in battle, and they get louder as they win.
- **Mechanical identity:** *momentum & endurance*. Vigil Champions snowball wins, sustain Focus, and are hardest to stop once they are singing.
- **The crack:** **Cantors** (the Hush must be ended at any cost, including burning the Margin) versus **Lanterns** (protect people *from* the Hush, not the doctrine).
- **Heroes:** Caska Vell, Maren Solt. **Villain:** High Cantor Absolom Reyne. **Unknown:** Lirae.

## 8. Unsolved things (the list writers pull from)

1. **The Unsinging.** Who struck the Fork? (§5)
2. **The eighth seal.** Seven signatories, eight seals.
3. **The third tine.** Missing since 2 BA. Every faction has accused every other faction of hiding it.
4. **The death of Ilsabet Brask.** Designer of the Fork, found dead in a locked bell tower in Carillon the night before the Unsinging. Suspects: her husband, the Empress, the Vigil, and a set of footprints that fade after three steps.
5. **Why ash still falls on Solenne.** Twenty-one years, and it hasn't stopped. It falls in the same pattern every day.
6. **The Hush.** Now 41 minutes. It grew four minutes last year. The Vigil's new prophecy says the song ends when the Hush becomes an hour.
7. **Streets in the Blank.** Vega Sorn's maps show the Blank's ruins being *rebuilt* — by no one.
8. **The empty portrait.** In the Vantaine gallery at Cinderhold hangs a portrait frame with nothing in it. No one remembers hanging it. Aurelle forbids its removal and can't say why.
9. **Firstfall.** What fell, why now, and why the star that went out was the one the Margin calls *the Twelfth Note*.

**◼ SEALED — the empty portrait.** The Hollow King was **Aldric Vantaine**, eldest child of Maelis, who defected to the twelfth city for love and became its king. When the city was unsung, so was he: his family forgot him. Aurelle erased her own brother. The Hollow King remembers only that he has a name and it was taken. When Aurelle and the Hollow King meet in a Clash, both of them feel grief they cannot explain. (Relationship ability: see Character Bible.)

**◼ SEALED — Lirae.** The fallen star was the twelfth city's note, held in the sky since the Overture. When the city was unsung, its note lost its anchor and began to fall — slowly, over twenty-one years. Lirae is that note, wearing a person. If she is *sung* — heard fully by someone Attuned — the twelfth city returns to the Threnody, and **everyone remembers everything**. Including who struck the Fork, who forged the dispatch, who stole the tine, and who the First Silence really is. That is why half the world wants her claimed, and the other half wants her silenced.

## 9. The political landscape at Firstfall

- **The Crown** needs a Clash victory at Coda to reclaim the Ashfields, and they need Lirae kept quiet — Aurelle's secret depends on it. Corvin wants her as a power source to rebuild the Fork.
- **The Quiet** officially claim jurisdiction over anything that fell from the sky. Unofficially, the First Silence wants Lirae silenced before she is sung.
- **The Hourwrights** want to toll Lirae — to cut a star into sellable hours. Ottavio also needs the truth about the third tine buried forever.
- **The Margin** found her first. The Hollow King believes she holds his name.
- **The Vigil** believe she is the Voice's answer to their prayers. High Cantor Absolom believes she must be *sung in Lanternfast*, and that the Margin must burn to prove the Vigil's devotion. Caska Vell believes she's a frightened person who needs protecting.

Under the Accord, a claim to a fallen star must be settled by Clash. **Firstfall is a tournament season: the Firstfall Claims.** Each week the world's Clashes (player activity) move the claim standings, but the story's outcome is authored.

## 10. Season 1 — FIRSTFALL (story arc outline)

1. **Act I — The Fall.** The star comes down. Everyone races into the Blank. The Margin already has her. First Claims Clashes.
2. **Act II — The Claims.** Corvin breaks the Accord and attacks a Margin camp outside of Clash — the first Accord violation in nineteen years. The Quiet send Silencers. Iver Hale must choose between his old oath to the Crown and his new one to the Quiet.
3. **Act III — The Singing.** Lirae begins to hum. Everyone near her starts remembering fragments: Aurelle dreams of an older brother; Mother Ink finds a name she can't read. The Hush grows by a full minute in one night.
4. **Finale — SOMETHING HAS ARRIVED.** The first World Boss: **The Rest** — a shape made of the Hush itself, drawn by the note's return. The Season ends with the world's factions fighting side by side against it, and Aurelle alone at the edge of the Blank, looking at a man with no name and her mother's eyes.

Unrevealed at season end: whether Lirae is sung. That's Season 2.

## 11. Premium currency naming

**Chimes.** It fits the world's language (bells, tolling) without sharing a name with a faction. Soft currency stays **Embers** (duplicates are *burned* into Embers — Crown ritual language, universally understood). Pack Tickets are **Writ Seals** in flavour text only; the UI says Pack Ticket.
